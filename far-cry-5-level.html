CASE STUDY · LEVEL DESIGN

Twilight Tropics

A tropical archipelago level built around a bounty hunt, where players can approach their objective through different routes, tactics and levels of risk.

Far Cry 5 Map Editor PC · 2024 Solo project 2 weeks · Half-time

My role: Level Designer

Overview Design Vision Level Structure Player Choice Supporting Areas Reflections
01 / OVERVIEW

A bounty hunt built around player choice

Twilight Tropics is a solo level-design project inspired by the tropical environments of the original Far Cry.

I created the level using the Far Cry 5 Map Editor to focus on spatial design, encounters and environmental storytelling rather than building gameplay mechanics from scratch.

The objective
Locate and eliminate a bounty target before escaping the archipelago. I wanted players to have multiple ways of completing this objective, encouraging exploration and different tactical approaches.

01

Player choice

Designed alternative routes and traversal opportunities to support different approaches to the objective.

02

Threat progression

Distributed enemy encounters across areas with different intended threat levels and playstyles.

03

Environmental design

Used terrain, sightlines and rewards to encourage exploration and influence player decisions.

02 / DESIGN VISION

Designing freedom of approach

I wanted the objective to remain clear without making the path toward it feel predetermined. My approach was to use recognizable landmarks, alternative routes and tactical opportunities to balance player guidance with freedom of exploration.

The Main Design Challenge

How can I guide players toward an assassination objective while allowing them to choose their own route and playstyle?

Give direction

Use recognizable landmarks, routes and tactical opportunities to help players understand where they can go and what they can do.

Preserve choice

Provide different ways to traverse the environment and approach encounters, allowing players to favor stealth, direct combat or exploration.

03 / LEVEL STRUCTURE

Four areas, different tactical opportunities

I divided the archipelago into four distinct areas. Each area has its own purpose, intended threat level and opportunities for different approaches.

The variation in enemy presence, traversal and available resources was intended to encourage players to evaluate their options rather than rely on a single playstyle.

AREA 01

Checkpoint

Low threat

A lightly guarded access point controlling the route to a zipline and maintaining radio contact with the complex.

Intended approach: Stealth
AREA 02

Mountain

Medium threat

A guarded radio tower with alternative ascent routes and a sniper vantage point overlooking the fort.

Intended approach: Stealth
AREA 03

Storage

High threat

A heavily guarded supply area containing ammunition, health supplies and boats that can be used to reach the fort.

Intended approach: Combat or stealth
AREA 04

Fort

Very high threat

The primary objective area containing the assassination target, with multiple possible entry points.

Intended approach: Combat or stealth

Threat levels represent my intended encounter intensity, not measured difficulty or a required order of progression.

04 / DESIGNING PLAYER CHOICE

One destination, two approaches

One of my main design goals was to encourage players to explore alternative routes rather than always following the most obvious path.

The Mountain provided an opportunity to experiment with this idea. Players can reach its radio tower by following the road or discovering a less obvious grappling route.

ROUTE 01 · DIRECT

The road

The road is the more obvious and safer route toward the summit.

It offers players a straightforward approach without requiring them to discover an alternative path.

ROUTE 02 · EXPLORATION

The grappling route

The grappling route presents a more demanding alternative located slightly away from the main path.

I used angled spatial guidance and partial concealment to encourage observant players to discover it.

My Intent

I wanted the grappling route to feel like a rewarding discovery without making it the only viable way forward. Players who prefer a safer approach can still follow the road.

The Mountain in practice

SHARED DESTINATION

The radio tower

Reaching the summit gives players access to a sniper rifle and a vantage point overlooking the fort.

However, the assassination target remains obscured from this position. Players can use the vantage point to engage other enemies, but must still consider how to approach the objective itself.

Wingsuit

Continue toward the fort using aerial traversal.

Backtrack

Return through the level and approach the objective differently.

The intent was to offer meaningful traversal choices while preserving a clear destination. Different routes provide different experiences without requiring the player to follow one predetermined solution.

05 / SUPPORTING AREAS

Preparing for and reaching the objective

While the Mountain focuses on traversal choices and tactical positioning, Storage and the Fort introduce other decisions about how to approach encounters and reach the assassination target.

AREA 03 · HIGH THREAT

Storage — supplies and transport

Storage is a heavily guarded supply area containing ammunition, health kits and armor. It also provides access to boats that can transport players toward the Fort.

I provided two ways to enter the area, supporting both a direct and a less confrontational approach.

Vehicle entry

Break through the gates with a vehicle for a direct approach.

Western wall

Use a fallen tree to climb over the wall instead.

AREA 04 · VERY HIGH THREAT

The Fort — the final objective

The Fort is a heavily guarded research facility containing the assassination target. It represents the primary objective of the bounty hunt.

I designed several ways to reach and enter the island so that players could choose their preferred approach.

Reaching the island

Swim, glide or travel by boat to reach the Fort.

Entering the Fort

Approach from the air, use the main gate, or enter through a pipe on the western side.

06 / REFLECTIONS

Designing beyond my own preferences

One of the challenges of level design is balancing the experience I personally find enjoyable with the different ways other players may prefer to approach a level.

What I learned

Designing an interesting route is not enough on its own. Alternative approaches should remain worthwhile, even when they do not match my own preferred way of playing.

What I would validate

I would test whether players notice the alternative routes, understand their options, and experience the intended differences in encounter difficulty.

Twilight Tropics taught me to think beyond individual encounters and consider how routes, resources and environmental guidance work together to support player choice.
