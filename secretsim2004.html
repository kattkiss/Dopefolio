01 / OVERVIEW

Turning a familiar computer into a puzzle

Secret Simulator 2004 was developed over two days for a game jam hosted by the local student association Gills. The theme was "Secret", and the games were judged by game development teachers from the University of Skövde.

Our approach was to make discovery itself the puzzle. Players explore the files of a simulated family computer to uncover a hidden game and find the login credentials required to access it.

Best Theme Winner

Secret Simulator 2004 received the Best Theme award, one of four award categories at the game jam.

02 / PLAYTESTING

When mystery becomes confusion

Playtesting revealed an interesting contrast. Players enjoyed exploring the simulated computer, but often struggled to determine whether they were making progress toward solving the puzzle.

What worked

  • Players found the simulated computer immersive.
  • Searching through files was an enjoyable part of the experience.
  • Players and judges who didn't succeed often returned for three or more attempts.

Where players struggled

  • Finding the correct login credentials was often difficult.
  • Players received very little feedback when they were on the right track.
  • The game did not always communicate which interactions behaved like a real operating system, creating confusion.
03 / REFLECTIONS

Making the challenge clearer, not easier

The playtesting feedback highlighted several ways we could have made the experience more intuitive without removing the challenge of finding the hidden game and its login credentials.

Provide feedback on partial progress

Players had little indication of whether they had entered the correct username or password. Providing visual confirmation when either field was correct would have helped players recognize their progress without revealing the full solution.

Communicate available interactions

The simulated operating system did not always communicate which interactions were available. For example, players needed clearer indications that windows could be dragged and what the shutdown button would do.

Keep the scope manageable

Simulating an operating system proved more difficult than anticipated. For future game jams, I would prioritize building a small, complete experience before expanding it with additional features.
