02 / DESIGN VISION

Make the narrative feel interactive

My goal was to create a visual novel that felt like a game, rather than reading a novel. I wanted players to pay attention to the dialogue and feel like their input had consequences.

The Main Design Challenge

How can mechanics encourage attention and meaningful decisions without interrupting the story or frustrating the player?

The design challenge guided me towards three systems: timed choices to add urgency, quick-time events to introduce a different form of interaction, and intoxication effects to connect the player's perception to both the narrative and their choices.

03 / GAMEPLAY SYSTEMS

How the mechanics support the goal

SYSTEM 01 · DIALOGUE

Time-sensitive choices

Urgent decisions should be just that, URGENT.

My intent

Discourage dialogue skimming and encourage players to engage with consequential choices.

Player-facing behavior

If the player does not respond before the timer expires, the game makes the choice for them.

Intended effect

More attentive reading, tension, and a stronger sense of consequence.

SYSTEM 02 · TIMING CHALLENGE

Quick-time events

A minigame that forces the player to respond with good timing rather than a dialogue selection.

My intent

Break up the narrative flow with a different interactive challenge, using performative uncertainty, while still supporting the story.

Player-facing behavior

A cursor travels from side to side. The player presses a button while it is within a target zone to progress.

Intended effect

Add variation to the challenge and encourage physical attention during significant moments.

SYSTEM 03 · VISUAL FEEDBACK

Levels of intoxication

Communicating the protagonist's level of intoxication through distorted visuals.

My intent

Reflect the protagonist's intoxication through the environment, reinforcing the consequences of drinking and sobering up.

Iteration

Early feedback showed that differences between intoxication levels were too subtle. I introduced more levels and strengthened the distortion.

04 / TECHNICAL DESIGN

Solving problems behind the systems

Making the narrative more interactive required more than designing new mechanics. I also had to connect Yarn Spinner with Unity gameplay systems, preserve narrative progress between sessions, and create visual feedback that worked with the game's rendering setup.

TECHNICAL CHALLENGE 01 · UNITY · YARN SPINNER

Connecting narrative and gameplay

Yarn Spinner controlled the story, but the dialogue also needed to trigger gameplay events such as changing the character's intoxication, start timed choices or QTEs, and narrative events needed to affect audio and presentation.

The challenge

Give the narrative designer control over gameplay events directly from Yarn dialogue, without needing to modify C# scripts.

My approach

I exposed functionality through separate Unity managers and controllers using custom Yarn commands and functions. Each controller remained responsible for its own gameplay behavior.

TECHNICAL CHALLENGE 02 · PERSISTENT PROGRESS

Saving narrative progress without separate scenes

I wanted players to be able to close the game and resume and keep their progress without splitting the story into separate Unity scenes. My solution was to use Yarn Spinner's dialogue nodes as checkpoints, storing their identifiers and additional progression data through Unity's PlayerPrefs.

TECHNICAL CHALLENGE 03 · SHADER GRAPH

Creating intoxication distortion without screen grabbing

To visually communicate intoxication, I wanted the environment to wobble as the protagonist drank. When an overlay-based shader tutorial didn't work with my project's background rendering setup, I developed an alternative approach.

05 / PLAYTESTING

What changed after feedback

OBSERVATION

Intoxication was too subtle

Early playtesting suggested the effects were not noticeable enough when going from one level to the other.

RESPONSE

Make the effect stronger

I introduced more levels of intoxication and strengthened the visual distortion considerably.

In other news, players did report appreciation of the added gameplay mechanics and that interaction made the narrative feel more engaging overall.
